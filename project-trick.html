Project Overview

During my NASA OSTEM Internship at the Johnson Space Center, my core objective was to modernize the visual fidelity of NASA's aerospace simulations. Specifically, I was tasked with creating detailed Unreal Engine 5 (UE5) environments that seamlessly synchronize with the existing example physics simulations hosted on the Trick Simulation Environment's GitHub repository.

The Trick-Unreal Connector (TUC) & Core Contributions

To bridge the gap between NASA's rigorous back-end physics engine and the front-end graphics of Unreal Engine 5, my workflow heavily relied on TUC (Trick-Unreal Connector), a specialized, not-yet-open-sourced plugin.

Beyond simply utilizing the tool, I actively contributed to the core TUC codebase. I developed and integrated the ability for TUC to ingest and process rotation matrices directly from the Trick backend. This structural math addition ensures that complex 3D spatial orientations calculated by the physics simulation map accurately onto the 3D actors rendering in real-time within Unreal Engine.

Asset Creation & Example Environments

To showcase the power of this integration, I developed 5 distinct UE5 environments, complete with optimized 3D assets I created in Blender. Each environment corresponds directly to a specific Trick simulation example, replacing legacy visuals with modern rendering pipelines.

SIM_satellite

Orbital mechanics and spatial visualization.

SIM_singlerigidbody

6-DOF physics and rotational dynamics.

SIM_splashdown

Water entry and buoyancy dynamics.

SIM_robot

Multi-joint articulation and robotics.

SIM_wheelbot

Rover kinematics and surface navigation.

Documentation & Official Tutorials

Building advanced simulation tools is only effective if other engineers can easily adopt the workflow. To ensure longevity and usability for my project, I heavily focused on technical writing alongside my development.

Official Trick Documentation Contribution

I authored an official, step-by-step tutorial published directly in the Trick Documentation How-To Guides. This tutorial guides users through the complete process of setting up a fresh Unreal Engine project and successfully linking it with a running Trick simulation. Additionally, I generated comprehensive Doxygen documentation for the codebase to support future plugin development.
